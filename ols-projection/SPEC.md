## The picture is exact

$y \in \mathbb R^n$ cannot be drawn, but $0$, $\mathbf 1$, $x$, $y$ and everything built from them ($\hat y$, $\bar y$,
$\hat\varepsilon$, $\bar\varepsilon$, $Xb$) lie in the subspace spanned by $\mathbf 1, x, y$, of dimension at most 3.
`frame(f)` gives an orthonormal basis $e_1, e_2, e_3$ of it: $e_1$ along $\hat y$, $(e_1, e_2)$ spanning the column space,
$e_3$ along $\hat\varepsilon$. The figure plots coordinates in this basis, so all lengths and angles are the true ones.
The column space is the floor $z = 0$, so Plotly's turntable rotation turns the figure around the normal of the plane.

## Model (`model.js`, `window.ProjectionModel`)

Columns of $X$: $\mathbf 1$ and $x$ (with an intercept) or $x$ alone. The projection is computed from an orthonormal basis
of the column space (Gram–Schmidt), so $\hat y$ and $\hat\varepsilon$ exist even when the columns are collinear and
$\hat\beta$ is not unique (then `beta` is `null`).

- `fit(x, y, intercept, withMatrices = true)` returns `n, cols, basis, rank, beta, yhat, resid, ybar, ebar, explained, tss, ess, rss, r2, r2ess, r2cor, XtE, onesIn, angleResid` and `P, M`.
- `frame(f)` returns `{ e, planar, to3 }`.
- `draws(seed, n)` (uniform $u_i$, standard normal $z_i$; the first draws do not depend on $n$) and
  `sample(d, beta0, beta1, sigma)`: $x_i = 10u_i$, $y_i = \beta_0 + \beta_1 x_i + \sigma z_i$.

Tests check, on fixed and random data ($n = 2$ to $50$) with and without an intercept: $\hat\beta$ against brute-force
minimisation of RSS and against $S_{xy}/S_{xx}$; $\hat y = X\hat\beta = Py$ with the explicit $P = X(X^TX)^{-1}X^T$;
$P, M$ symmetric and idempotent, $PM = 0$, trace $P$ = rank; $X^T\hat\varepsilon = 0$; Pythagoras; $RSS(b) = RSS + |X(\hat\beta - b)|^2$;
with an intercept $TSS = ESS + RSS$ and $R^2 = 1 - RSS/TSS = ESS/TSS = \mathrm{cor}(\hat y, y)^2$; without an intercept the
decomposition fails in general; the collinear case; the frame keeps all lengths and inner products; the simulated data.

## Page

Header: eyebrow "Regression basics", title "Regression as a Projection", link back to the index.

### Controls
1. How to read this.
2. Model: intercept on/off; the model, $\hat\beta$ and the true $\beta$.
3. Data: sliders $n$ (3–60, default 20), $\beta_0$ (default 1), $\beta_1$ (default 0.5), $\sigma$ (default 1.5);
   buttons New sample, Default (seed 4, $R^2 \approx 0.6$), Perfect fit ($\sigma = 0$), No relation ($\beta_1 = 0$).
   Points dragged in the scatter plot stay where they were put until a new sample or a preset.
4. Layers: the mean ($\bar y$, $\bar\varepsilon$, $\hat y - \bar y$; on), grid on the plane (on), right-angle marks (on),
   columns $\mathbf 1$ and $x$ (off), "try another fit $Xb$" (off) with sliders $b_0, b_1$ and a button $b = \hat\beta$.

### 3D figure (Plotly)
The column space as a shaded plane with a grid (a line when it is one-dimensional), the origin, arrows $y$ (black),
$\hat y$ (blue), $\hat\varepsilon$ (red), $\bar y$ (green), $\bar\varepsilon$ (yellow), the segment $\hat y - \bar y$ (purple),
a right-angle mark at $\hat y$, optionally the columns and $Xb$. No axes (they have no meaning in the slice).
Equal scales on all axes. Views: **Like the slide**, **Look down on the plane**, **Edge-on**; zoom buttons; the mouse wheel
scrolls the page instead of zooming.

### Side panels
- Scatter plot of $(x_i, y_i)$ with the fitted line, $\bar y$ and the residuals; points can be dragged.
- The triangle $\bar y$, $\hat y$, $y$ drawn flat with squares on its sides: TSS = ESS + RSS.
- Key numbers: $n$, $\hat\beta$, $|y|$, $|\hat y|$, $|\hat\varepsilon|$, $X^T\hat\varepsilon$, the angle between $\hat y$ and $\hat\varepsilon$,
  TSS, ESS, RSS, $R^2$, $\mathrm{cor}(\hat y, y)^2$, $|y - Xb|^2 = RSS + |\hat y - Xb|^2$; $P$ and $M$ (shown for $n \le 6$)
  with checks of symmetry and idempotence.
